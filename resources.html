Curious about the words behind the tool? Here is a short primer on quires, plus hand-picked links to dig deeper and to try more hands-on projects.

What is a quire?

A quire (also called a gathering or signature) is a set of leaves made by folding one or more sheets of paper or parchment together. Each fold doubles the number of leaves: fold a sheet once and you get a folio (2 leaves, 4 pages); fold it again for a quarto (4 leaves, 8 pages); again for an octavo, and so on.

Because a whole sheet is printed flat and only folded afterwards, the pages are not laid out in reading order on the sheet. Working out which page goes where — so everything reads correctly once folded and trimmed — is called imposition. Several quires are then stacked and sewn together to build the codex: the bound book we still read today. Quire Maker lets you watch that whole process happen from a passage of your own text.
