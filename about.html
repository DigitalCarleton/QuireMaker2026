QuireMaker is designed to allow students to experiment with producing their own quires— some of the basic components of a book. By entering their chosen text and selecting elements of the format, they can generate correctly imposed sheets ready to fold into gatherings. It is a teaching tool born in the Book Studies classroom that aims to give students hands-on experience with the material reality of early modern bookmaking.

How it started

Pierre wanted a tool that would help students understand collation and what the physical creation and assembly of books involved — not just theoretically but experientially. Tom Feiler, a colleague in ITS, quickly built a first version in Reason. Students reached it through a simple web link, typed or pasted their text, and had it render as folio–, quarto–, or octavo– ready PDFs.

The app was used successfully for a few years, until Reason was deprecated and it stopped working. The idea, though, seemed worth pursuing, so the project came to ILiADS to be revived, reimagined, and rebuilt from scratch.

A short timeline

  • ca. 2020 Pierre proposes a digital quire tool; Tom (ITS) builds the first version in Reason.
  • 2020–2022 Students use it via a web link, rendering text as folio, quarto, and octavo.
  • ca. 2023 Tom leaves and Reason is deprecated; the tool stops working.
  • 2026 The project is brought to ILiADS at Vassar to build a new, dependency-free tool — the one you are using now.

Why it matters

Book Studies courses are on the rise again at Carleton thanks to the collaborative, interdisciplinary work of (among others) faculty, Research and Instruction librarians, academic technologists, and students. Our hope is that QuireMaker can be widely used across those classes, in Special Collections, and elsewhere — a free, shared, approachable way for anyone to see how a printed sheet becomes a book.
