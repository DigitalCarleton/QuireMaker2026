About the Project

Quire Maker helps students understand quires by hand: paste in text, pick a format, and generate correctly imposed pages ready to fold into a real gathering. It is a teaching tool born in the Book Studies classroom.

How it started

Pierre wanted a tool that would help students truly understand quires — not just read about them, but paste text into a digital tool and get PDFs formatted the correct way, ready to fold. A friend in ITS, Tom, quickly built a first version in Reason that worked for about two years. Students reached it through a simple web link, typed or pasted their text, and had it render as folio, quarto, and octavo.

That original tool did not handle images well, and when Tom moved on and Reason was deprecated, it stopped working. The idea, though, was too good to lose — so the project came to ILiADS to be revived and rebuilt from scratch.

A short timeline

  • ca. 2020 Pierre proposes a digital quire tool; Tom (ITS) builds the first version in Reason.
  • 2020–2022 Students use it via a web link, rendering text as folio, quarto, and octavo.
  • ca. 2023 Tom leaves and Reason is deprecated; the tool stops working.
  • 2026 The project is brought to ILiADS at Vassar to build a new, dependency-free tool — the one you are using now.

Why it matters

Book Studies courses are on the rise again thanks to the work of faculty and the library. Our hope is that Quire Maker can be widely used across those classes — a shared, approachable way for anyone to see how a printed sheet becomes a book. We keep asking the practical question: how would instructors like George and Jackie actually use this with their students?
