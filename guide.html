In bookmaking, “imposition” refers to the process of arranging pages in the printing process so that, once assembled, the pages appear in the correct order and orientation. QuireMaker automates the laying out of pages onto a printed sheet, while allowing users to experiment with different formats and signature sizes. With QuireMaker, you can control size, format, page numbers, and signatures, while seeing exactly which page number lands where, how sheets must be folded, and which way is up.

Give it a try! The tool preloads with sample text, so you can click Impose & preview right away to see how it works. Now try it with your own text.

Step by step

  1. Pick a format. Using the Format menu, you can choose to create a Folio (or ‘bifolium’) sheet, which would be folded once, resulting in two leaves and thus four pages. Each additional fold doubles the number of leaves and pages. QuireMaker allows for Folio (2°), Quarto (4°), Octavo (8°), or Sextodecimo (16°) sheets to be rendered.
  2. Name the Signature. The Signature letter (usually “A”) is the printer’s label for this quire.
  3. Set the type Size and Typeface (you may want to experiment with these as you explore different signature sizes).
  4. Select/deselect What to Show on the Page to show/hide page numbers, signature marks, catchwords, and paragraph breaks (which will treat blank lines as new paragraphs).
  5. Add Copy text. Type or paste into the Copy text box. Large amounts of text will spread onto as many gatherings as needed.
  6. Impose & preview. Press the gold button to watch the sheet fold into whatever kind of quire you selected, then scroll to Finished pages to read them in order.
  7. Explore the formes. Click any page to spotlight where it sits on both sides of the sheet. Numbers flagged 180° are printed upside down — that is normal, as they will come right side up once folded correctly.
  8. Print. Hit print, choose duplex, and flip on the long edge. Fold along the creases, and nest the sheets to make a bifolium, ternion, quaternion, or other kind of quire.

Tips & troubleshooting

My text made lots of blank pages. Text is always rounded up to a whole gathering, so the leftover leaves at the end sit blank — exactly as a real printer would leave them.To use more or less space, you might adjust the font size.

Reading the folding theatre. Use Fold and Unfold to move one crease at a time, and Turn over to flip the sheet and see the reverse.

Printing looks off. Make sure your printer dialog is set to A4 landscape with margins at zero, and that duplex flips on the long edge.
